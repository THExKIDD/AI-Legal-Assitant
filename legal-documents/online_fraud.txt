ONLINE FRAUD

Online fraud occurs when digital methods are used dishonestly to obtain money, information, account access, or another benefit.

Examples may include phishing links, fake shopping websites, payment scams, impersonation, fraudulent investment schemes, fake customer-care calls, and unauthorized online transactions.

A victim should act quickly after discovering suspected online fraud.

The person should preserve screenshots, transaction IDs, bank statements, phone numbers, email addresses, website links, messages, and other available digital evidence.

If financial information or an account may have been compromised, the victim should promptly contact the relevant bank or payment service provider and take appropriate steps to secure the account.

The incident may also be reported through the appropriate cybercrime reporting or law-enforcement mechanism.

Passwords and other account credentials should be changed when necessary, and compromised accounts should be secured.

For example, if a person enters banking information on a fake website and an unauthorized transaction occurs, the person should preserve the evidence and promptly report the transaction and incident through appropriate channels.

The appropriate reporting and legal procedure depends on the nature of the fraud and applicable law.
