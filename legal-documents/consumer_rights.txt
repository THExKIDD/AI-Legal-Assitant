CONSUMER RIGHTS

A consumer is a person who purchases goods or uses services for consideration.

Consumer protection law provides rights and remedies to consumers when they face problems with goods or services.

Important consumer rights include the right to safety, right to information, right to choose, right to be heard, right to seek redressal, and right to consumer awareness.

A consumer may have a complaint when goods are defective, services are deficient, unfair trade practices are used, or the consumer is charged improperly.

A consumer should keep important evidence such as bills, invoices, receipts, warranty documents, emails, messages, and other records related to the purchase.

Depending on the problem, the consumer may first contact the seller or service provider and request a solution.

If the dispute is not resolved, the consumer may use the appropriate consumer grievance or dispute-redressal mechanism.

For example, if a customer purchases an electronic product and it is defective, the customer can contact the seller or manufacturer and seek an appropriate remedy according to the applicable consumer protection rules.

The exact remedy and procedure depend on the facts of the case and the applicable law.
